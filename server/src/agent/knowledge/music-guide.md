# Music Guide for WordChord Users

## Capo Guide

A capo raises the pitch of the guitar without changing the chord shapes you play. The capo field in WordChord lets you record which fret the capo is on so collaborators know exactly how to play the song.

### Capo Key Conversion Table

If you play these chord shapes with the capo on the listed fret, the sounding key changes as follows:

| Shape Key | Capo 1  | Capo 2   | Capo 3  | Capo 4   | Capo 5 |
|-----------|---------|----------|---------|----------|--------|
| C         | C#/Db   | D        | D#/Eb   | E        | F      |
| G         | G#/Ab   | A        | A#/Bb   | B        | C      |
| D         | D#/Eb   | E        | F       | F#/Gb    | G      |
| A         | A#/Bb   | B        | C       | C#/Db    | D      |
| E         | F       | F#/Gb    | G       | G#/Ab    | A      |
| Am        | A#m/Bbm | Bm       | Cm      | C#m/Dbm  | Dm     |
| Em        | Fm      | F#m/Gbm  | Gm      | G#m/Abm  | Am     |

**Example:** If a song is in the key of B but you want to use easy open G shapes, put the capo on fret 4 and play G chord shapes — the guitar sounds in B.

## Chord Progressions by Genre

These are common progressions used by genre. Roman numerals refer to scale degrees. For a song in C major: I=C, II=D, III=E, IV=F, V=G, VI=Am, VII=Bdim.

### Pop
- I – V – vi – IV (the most common pop progression: C – G – Am – F)
- I – IV – V – I (classic resolution: C – F – G – C)
- vi – IV – I – V (minor feel start: Am – F – C – G)

### Rock
- I – IV – V (blues-rock backbone: C – F – G)
- I – bVII – IV (borrowed flat-seven: C – Bb – F)
- i – bVII – bVI – bVII (natural minor rock: Am – G – F – G)

### Blues
- 12-bar blues: I – I – I – I – IV – IV – I – I – V – IV – I – V
- In A: A7 – A7 – A7 – A7 – D7 – D7 – A7 – A7 – E7 – D7 – A7 – E7

### Jazz
- ii – V – I (the jazz fundamental: Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7)
- I – vi – ii – V (turnaround: Cmaj7 – Am7 – Dm7 – G7)

### Folk / Singer-Songwriter
- I – V – vi – iii – IV (extended pop: C – G – Am – Em – F)
- I – IV – vi – V (C – F – Am – G)

### Israeli / Mediterranean
- i – bVII – bVI – V (Phrygian-flavored cadence common in Israeli rock: Am – G – F – E)
- i – IV – i – V (Am – Dm – Am – E)

## Song Structure Guide

WordChord sections map directly to standard song forms. Here is how to use them effectively.

### Verse–Chorus (Most common)
The verse tells the story, the chorus repeats the emotional peak. Use at least two verses before the final chorus. Structure: Verse – Chorus – Verse – Chorus – Bridge – Chorus.

### AABA (Standard / Jazz form)
Four equal sections, three identical (A) and one contrasting (B). The B section is called the "bridge" or "middle eight." Common in older pop and jazz standards.

### Through-Composed
No repeated sections — the song keeps changing. Suits storytelling songs. Use the Custom section type for each unique part.

### Tips for Section Order in WordChord
- Start with an Intro if the song has a riff or instrumental lead-in
- End with an Outro if the song fades or has a tag ending
- Use Bridge to provide contrast before the final chorus
- Drag sections to reorder them — no need to retype content
